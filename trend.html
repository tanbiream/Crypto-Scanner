Trend Test: 200-day average rule (BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP)

Version . Daily candles from Coinbase Exchange public data. Analysis only, no trading.
Pre-registered rules and bar, written before any results (Nov 24, 2026):
Rule (per coin): hold when the previous day's close is above its 200-day simple average, otherwise stay in cash. Trades at the next day's open. Round-trip cost 1.0% (0.5% per side).
Data: only the latest continuous stretch (no gap longer than 3 missing days). A coin counts toward the bar only with at least 3 years of it; others are informational. Combined result: equal-weight portfolio of the included coins, rebalanced daily.
Compared with: buy-and-hold of the same coins (one entry cost). This tests whether the rule is a sensible risk-managed way to hold them, not whether it beats buy-and-hold on return.
The rule meets the bar only if ALL hold: (1) at least 2 included coins and at least 30 completed trades in total; (2) combined annualized return after cost above zero; (3) at least 2 included coins individually above zero; (4) combined maximum drawdown at most 60% of buy-and-hold's AND a higher return-to-drawdown ratio (annualized return divided by maximum drawdown) than buy-and-hold; (5) combined annualized return above zero in BOTH halves of the period AND also with 150-day and 250-day averages (robustness only, not tuning). A pass is provisional: these are today's large survivors (selection bias), and the rule needs forward confirmation. Any change becomes a new version (t2).

Known biases: BTC, ETH, SOL and XRP are chosen because they are large today, so holding them looks better than it would have to someone choosing earlier (selection and survivorship bias). Fills are assumed exactly at the open, which is optimistic; 1.0% round trip is meant to cover fees and slippage on large coins, and real costs may differ. Prices come from Coinbase and differ slightly from other venues. Returns are open to open. Daily rebalancing of the combined portfolio is a simplification. A bull-market-heavy history favors holding; a long bear market favors cash. Nothing here is financial advice, and a pass would not be proof.
